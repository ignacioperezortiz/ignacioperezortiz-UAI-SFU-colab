The two regions. “Whole network” shows the (P, Q) pairs exactly as the solver returns them, measured at the PCC, the feeder head: they say where the feeder sits. Their position is set by the passive imbalance of the whole network — load minus PV plus losses — which travels some 3.3 MW between midday and the evening and which nobody controls within the service slot. “Batteries only” subtracts that reference point: it says what the batteries can do about it. Same shape and same size, different origin.

Why the distinction matters here. It is Tulio's criterion in results/TR9_FOR_rolling.md: the absolute regions of the nine transformers overlap between −2.16 and −2.29 MW and cannot be told apart, while their widths differ by 3.7×. Only the deviation view separates them. His conclusion: the deviation region for allocation and dispatch, the absolute one for network feasibility. Fairness is allocation, so the default view here is the deviation one.

Careful with the area. Scaling a 2-D region by a factor γ scales its area by γ². An area loss of 87 % amounts to a linear shrink to ~36 %, not to 13 %.

How it is drawn. For each of 12 directions, the directional sweep solves for the point of the region that reaches furthest in that direction; joining those 12 points gives the outline. Since fairness is a constraint, the region can only shrink: the three are always nested, verified at all 576 vertices.
